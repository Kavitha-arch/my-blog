Anamika's Journey Through the Kleshas

Spiritual Psychology

From Reaction to Radiance:

A practical exploration of how hidden egoic conditioning transforms everyday interactions into opportunities for awakening through the lens of Patanjali's Kleshas and the energetic progression of Verse 13.

Methodology NoteBeginning at Verse 13 (Earlier verses to be retroactively mapped)

The Architecture of the Inner Mirror

Mauna was once Anamika's closest friend who is now family, but over time they drifted apart. The reasons no longer matter—perhaps Anamika made mistakes, perhaps Mauna did, or perhaps both played a part. What matters is the present moment. They are not trying to rebuild their friendship, yet circumstances require them to continue living together for a time. The challenge, therefore, is not how to become friends again, but how to find inner peace and remain spiritually grounded while sharing the same space.

The Protagonist: Anamika (“The Nameless”)

Her name symbolizes the ultimate spiritual trap: we grow deeply attached to our names, which represent our faces, bodies, and educations as a rigid identity. The truth is that none of these are our true identity. “I AM” is the only absolute truth.

The Antagonist: Mauna (“Silence”)

Her name is explicitly chosen to represent that the demon does not exist externally. The demon is silent. We, within our own heads, are the ones giving a loud voice to an asura which actually, or probably, is not even saying or thinking what we imagine.

1. The Anatomy of Anamika's Internal Crisis

Anamika is trapped in a classic spiritual split. Because she is a dedicated seeker, she meditates, studies philosophy, and sincerely attempts to invoke the expansive presence of Durga.

The scenarios unfold now as follows with verse 13 explained in their context and continue in verse 14 onwards

However, the moment she enters the shared kitchen and encounters Mauna, a physical shift occurs. Her posture stiffens, her throat tightens, her energy becomes cold, and a stream of silent, defensive thoughts begins to flow:

“I hate Mauna... she is bad!!! I hate everything about her, even what she is doing now... How long do I have to live and share things with her...”

Whether Anamika's assessment of Mauna is factually correct or not is ultimately irrelevant to her spiritual evolution. The real battlefield is not Mauna's behavior.

It is the fact that Anamika has allowed her own inner state to become a hostage to another person's presence.

By reacting with hidden rudeness and allowing her energy to shift, Anamika temporarily loses her anchor as the Unconditioned Self. She drops from the state of a Bliss-Radiator into a state of personal friction.

2. Phase One: Identifying the Hidden Kleshas

To move toward the serene, unconditioned energy described in Verse 13, Anamika first uses Patanjali's teachings as a mirror to uncover the psychological roots of her energetic shift.

Layer One — Asmita (The Spiritualized Ego)

Anamika's deepest blind spot is Asmita. Her ego has constructed an identity around being “the spiritual seeker.”

When Mauna behaves in ways Anamika considers incorrect, the mind instantly creates a moral hierarchy:

“I am trying to remain pure. Mauna is disturbing the environment.”

Ironically, this very judgment feeds the ego. While believing she is protecting spirituality, Anamika unknowingly generates the same dense emotional atmosphere she attributes to Mauna.

Layer Two — Raga & Dvesha

Anamika clings (Raga) to an idealized image of a peaceful apartment while simultaneously resisting (Dvesha) the reality of another human being's conditioning.

Her peace collapses because reality refuses to conform to her inner expectations.

Layer Three — Abhinivesha

Beneath every justification lies a subtler fear:

“But I am right.”

This is Abhinivesha—the ego's instinct for psychological survival. If Anamika admits that her suffering comes from her own reaction rather than Mauna's behavior, the identity of being morally correct begins to dissolve.

3. Phase Two: Entering the Unconditioned Energy of Verse 13

Once Anamika witnesses her ego without self-condemnation, Verse 13 becomes a practical roadmap for returning to her true nature.

Step One — The Unstoppable Flow

मत्तमतङ्गजराजपते अविरलगण्डगलन्मदमेदुर...

Instead of entering the kitchen defensively, Anamika invokes the archetype of the sovereign elephant—a symbol of overwhelming, unstoppable spiritual vitality.

“My true nature is an ocean of awareness. It is so vast that Mauna's behavior cannot diminish it.”

Her presence softens. Instead of tightening inward, awareness begins overflowing outward.

Step Two — Resting in the Ocean of Grace

त्रिभुवनभूषण भूतकलानिधि रूपपयोनिधि राजसुते...

Anamika recognizes that she is meant to become the ornament of her environment—not by changing others, but by embodying beauty and grace regardless of circumstances.

She no longer sees Mauna as an obstacle but as another human being moving through her own conditioning.

Step Three — Becoming the Source of Magnetism

अयि सुदतीजनलालसमानसमोहनमन्मथराजसुते...

This marks the decisive transformation. Instead of chasing peace or trying to reform another person, Anamika purifies her own inner atmosphere.

Her presence naturally becomes magnetic. Peace is no longer something she seeks from the environment—it radiates from within her.

She no longer experiences an energetic disturbance when Mauna enters the room. Instead, her own state becomes the dominant frequency in the apartment.

By refusing to participate in the battle of egos, Anamika ceases to be merely a seeker attempting to survive a difficult roommate. She becomes an unshakable source of bliss that silently transforms the space around her.
