Verse 17: विजितसहस्रकरैक


17. Maa Durga: Devi grants what one sincerely seeks : cultivate wisdom before asking.

विजितसहस्रकरैक सहस्रकरैक सहस्रकरैकनुते

कृतसुरतारक सङ्गरतारक सङ्गरतारक सूनुसुते ।

सुरथसमाधि समानसमाधि समाधिसमाधि सुजातरते ।

जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥ १७ ॥

17.1: O Divine Mother, Your incomparable power and splendor surpass and triumph over even those of the mightiest thousand-armed celestial beings, while You are reverently praised and worshiped by innumerable devotees their thousand hands raised in devotion.!

17.2: O Divine Mother, You rescued the gods from the terror of the demon Tarakasura, You are the ultimate savior who delivers victory in the battles of life, and You are the Mother of that glorious divine warrior son (Lord Kartikeya)! who destroyed Tarakasura

17.3: O Divine Mother, You are the One who forcefully and intensely elevated both King Suratha (the distressed ruler) and Samadhi the merchant (the broken-hearted seeker) into the exact same ultimate state of unshakeable equanimity and grace!

17.4: O Divine Mother Victory, victory to You, O Slayer of Mahishasura , O Beautiful-haired One , O Daughter of the Mountain (Parvati) .


विजितसहस्रकरैकसहस्रकरैकनुते
  • The Two Meanings of 'Thousand Arms' (Sahasrakara) 1: In the kitchen, the room is split between two distinct forces. On one side, Anamika is performing her Puja to Maa Durga, trying to cultivate the pristine, blinding light of the Sun (Sahasrakara - rays). On the other side, Mauna is actively moving her physical hands (Sahasrakara - arms), cooking fish—a smell that Anamika naturally detests and finds highly invasive.
  • The Conquest of Equanimity (Vijita) By repeating this line, Anamika realizes that the Mother's true presence completely eclipses and conquers (Vijita) even the raw, blinding glare of the sun. The intense smell of the fish and the fragrance of the puja incense are both happening within the Mother’s space.
  • Anamika lets go of her rigid preference She allows the sunlight streaming through the window to ground her. She surrenders her aversion. Her mind becomes so expansive that the pungent smell of the fish no longer threatens her inner sanctuary. She stays anchored in her puja, smiling peacefully, while Mauna continues cooking. The sheer power of Anamika’s non-reactive equanimity completely clears the psychological tension in the air.
2. कृतसुरतारक सङ्गरतारक सूनुसुते —
  • The Internal Tarakasura As Mauna cooks the fish, the highly invasive, pungent smell fills the kitchen, threatening to disrupt Anamika's sacred morning routine. This deep-seated aversion and irritation is Anamika's internal Tarakasura (the demon of reactivity). It attempts to hijack her peace, tempting her to look at Mauna with judgment, anger, or victimization.
  • The Savior in the Kitchen Battle (Saṅgara-Tāraka) Anamika mentally chants this line. She realizes that the true 'battlefield' (Saṅgara) is not the physical kitchen floor, but her own nervous system. Instead of fighting Mauna or trying to change the environment, she surrenders her sensory aversion to the Mother. The Mother acts as Saṅgara-Tāraka, lifting Anamika's awareness cleanly above the frequency of disgust and judgment.
  • Birthing the Inner Divine Warrior (Sūnu-Sute) Just as the Mother gave birth to Lord Kartikeya to destroy the demon, Anamika's surrender births a powerful inner warrior: Unshakable Spiritual Discernment. Bathed in the soft rays of morning sunlight entering through the window, Anamika continues her puja with a serene smile. The smell of the fish and the fragrance of her incense exist in the exact same air, but her mind is completely anchored. She remains a sanctuary of pure equanimity, effortlessly neutralizing the room's friction without uttering a single word.
3. सुरथसमाधिसमप्रसभेषु —
  • King Suratha and Merchant Samadhi सुरथ means a skilled charioteer and Merchant समाधि means a focused mind. Both are restless and unhappy, and therefore meet Sage Medhas (meaning Buddhi—the ultimate inner guide), who gives them the Devi’s Beeja Mantra. They both meditate intensely on the banks of the river. Finally, Maa Durga appears and offers each a boon. King Suratha asks for the return of his physical kingdom and for it to be imperishable in his next life, while Samadhi asks for the Supreme Knowledge (Jnana) that will completely dissolve the bondage of worldly existence.
  • King Suratha's Representation He perfectly represents Anamika's current state: the righteous ruler trying to keep her spiritual space pure, structured, and strictly ‘correct’ (Sattvik), but feeling deeply violated, anxious, and rigid when external conditions cross her boundaries.
  • The Reign of Absolute Equanimity As she chants this verse, Anamika's rigid resistance snaps completely. Her Chitta guides her to prays to the Goddess whose grace helps Anamika in elevating her consciousness to this Sama state, which completely neutralizes the emotional tension, turning a volatile roommate conflict into a silent temple of inner freedom, thus gracefully moving towards the ultimate liberation of Merchant Samadhi. She looks at the bright beams of morning sunlight cutting through the heavy, pungent smoke. She stops labeling the kitchen as a battlefield. Her mind expands so wide that it holds both the sacred chanting of her puja and the crackle of Mauna's frying pan with total neutrality. A profound, unshakeable calm settles over her face.

✍️ रक्ष रक्ष जगन्माते सर्वशक्ते जय दुर्गे।🙏

By identifying that King Suratha’s desire for an 'imperishable kingdom' is actually the ego's hidden trap—the desperate, exhausting pursuit of permanent human perfection and constant happiness—you show that true victory only happens when we transition into Merchant Samadhi's space: the complete surrender of preferences, leading to the ultimate state of Moksha as we live.

Step 1: The Battlefield of Reactivity (Kṛta-sura-tāraka-saṅgara-tāraka-sūnu-sute)You correctly note that the first stage of spiritual life is active warfare. Anamika is in a battle with her own ignorant mind, which demands perfection from Mauna. To survive, she invokes the energy of the Mother's son, Lord Kartikeya. She uses His spear (Vel)—representing sharp, laser-focused spiritual discernment (Viveka)—to pierce through her internal Tarakasura (the demon of reactivity). She rides His Peacock, symbolizing that she has caught, tamed, and mounted her own pride, vanity, and desire to control her environment.

Step 2: The Trap of Righteous Control (Suratha)Once the mind is controlled, the seeker enters the King Suratha phase. Suratha is a great, righteous king, but he is trapped in a subtle illusion: he cannot accept failure, rejection, or messiness. He wants a perfect, unchanging kingdom. In the kitchen, this is Anamika wanting a perfectly Sattvik space, completely safe from the smell of fish. You brilliantly identify this as the human hope for 'constant happiness.' But an unchanging kingdom is an illusion of the ego; external weather will always change.

Step 3: The Ascent to Living Liberation (Samadhi)Finally, the seeker transitions into Merchant Samadhi. Samadhi has lost everything externally, which forces him to look for the absolute, unchanging Truth. He stops demanding that the world change. He seeks Equanimity (Sama). When Anamika drops her resistance to the fish smell and rests in her Puja, she embodies Samadhi. By holding both the sacred incense and the pungent cooking with total neutrality, she becomes the true King of both her inner mind and the outer world. She achieves Moksha right there in the shared kitchen—completely free while alive (Jivanmukti).
