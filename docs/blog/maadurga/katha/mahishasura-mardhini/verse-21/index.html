Verse 21: अयि मयि दीन दयालुतया


21. Maa Durga - Symbol of Courage , Kindness and Compassion, Bring this Mind to rest in the Heart at your Feet

अयि मयि दीन दयालुतया कृपयैव त्वया भवितव्यमुमे

अयि जगतो जननी कृपयासि यथासि तथानुमितासिरते ।

यदुचितमत्र भवत्युररीकुरुतादुरुतापमपाकुरुते

जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥ २१ ॥

21.1: O Uma, O Divine Mother, toward me, who am humble and helpless, You should indeed act with compassion for the afflicted. It is only through Your grace alone that I can find refuge and upliftment.

21.2: O Mother of the entire universe, You are truly the embodiment of compassion. Just as You really are by Your very nature, so You are known, experienced, and lovingly adored by Your devotees.

21.3: Whatever You consider appropriate for me, may You graciously accept and grant. Please remove all my intense sorrow, suffering, and afflictions through Your boundless mercy.

21.4: Victory! Victory to You! O Slayer of the buffalo demon Mahishasura, O Beautifully tressed One, O Daughter of the Mountain.

21.1: O Mother Uma, because You are naturally full of compassion toward the weary and helpless, You must take complete charge of me solely through Your unconditional, spontaneous grace.

21.2: O Mother of the universe, You are the very embodiment of compassion; Your infinite presence is clearly inferred through the intricate design of all creation.

21.3: O Supreme Lady, please accept and execute whatever You deem right for me, and completely dissolve the heavy, burning afflictions and anxieties weighing down my mind.


Life and its Challenges - Surrender and DEvotion.
  • The Bundle of Conditioning When we are born, we start with the bundle of the biological conditioning of our genes. As we grow, the bundle gets heavier due to our own acquired habits, both good and bad, and due to the environment we live in
  • The Blindness of Youth Minor issues look huge for lack of awareness. However, the strength of youth, the joys of new achievements, and hormones ride us through the challenges that come, leaving us with no time to reflect.
  • The Sudden Awakening Then suddenly, life begins to slow down. Reality hits you, and you cry out to the Goddess to save you. You will swear surrender, but alas, the ego is not an easy one to bow down and die.
  • The Tigress and Her Cub Meditation and awareness are to be cultivated as early as possible with daily devotion and prayers. Bhakti (loving devotion) is for the ones who are unable to dedicate themselves whole-heartedly to Jnana (the strict path of analytical wisdom). Therefore, in this verse, the devotee begs the Mother to save her and hold on to her like a tigress holds her cub. She is afraid that she is not committed or strong enough yet. The Mother's grace and compassion are ever-present, but are you ready to receive them? For grace to come through, you have to become empty. Muscles can be built, but the weights have to be lifted.

✍️ रक्ष रक्ष जगन्माते सर्वशक्ते जय दुर्गे।🙏

The Weighted Vest of Conditioning:Anamika looks back at her life journey and finally understands the heavy layout of human existence. When we enter this world, we start with a heavy bundle of biological conditioning etched into our genes. As we grow, that bundle becomes a suffocating weight due to our own acquired habits and the rigid expectations of society. In her youth, the surge of hormones, new achievements, and raw energy carried her through challenges without reflection. But as life slows down, the stark reality hits: the perfectionist ego cannot save her. It only makes her suffer by demanding an impossible, sterile reality.

The Failure of Mechanical Performance:She admits her own human limitations with radical vulnerability. She sits on her meditation mat, chanting mantras, but her mind still wavers. It drifts to her chores, her anxieties, and her ongoing friction with Mauna. She realizes that trying to aggressively force her mind into a state of perfection (Jnana) is just another trick of the ego.

The Shift to Cosmic Surrender (Urarīkurutāt):Instead of panicking over her wandering mind, Anamika completely drops her defenses. She stops trying to be the 'perfect seeker.' She adopts the attitude of a vulnerable cub. She screams out to the Mother: 'I am not strong enough to climb to You yet. I am distracted, conditioned, and flawed. Therefore, You must hold onto me. Pick me up by the scruff of my neck like a tigress holds her cub, and carry me across this river.'.

Emptying the Vessel to Receive Grace:She understands your profound law of effort and surrender: Muscles can be built, but the weights have to be lifted. The effort is simply showing up on the mat, building daily devotion, and choosing awareness over reactivity. The surrender is becoming completely empty so that the Mother's intelligence can flow through her.

The Final Peaceful Horizon:Anamika opens her eyes. The kitchen is still warm, the stove is still crackling, and Mauna is still quiet. But the burning heat of her internal anxiety (Uru-tāpam) has been completely blown away. She steps out onto her swing under the full moon, looking at the solitary Shiva Lingam standing unshakeable in the middle of the flowing river. She smiles a genuine, radiant smile. She may not have achieved an artificial, sterile perfection, but her mind is beautifully, permanently rested at the Mother’s feet inside her own heart. The stardust has returned to the stars.
