Verse 7: अयि निजहुङ्कृति मात्र


7. Maa Durga - Destroyed Dhumralochana , Rakthabeeja, Shumbha-Nishumbha

अयि निजहुङ्कृति मात्रनिराकृत धूम्रविलोचन धूम्रशते ।

समरविशोषित शोणितबीज समुद्भवशोणित बीजलते ॥

शिवशिवशुम्भ निशुम्भमहाहव तर्पितभूत पिशाचरते ।

जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥ ७ ॥

7.1: O Divine Mother, who by a mere sound of Your sacred roar destroyed the demon Dhumralochanainto hundred smoke particles.

7.2: O Mother, who dried up the battlefield where Raktabīja and his countless blood-born duplicates arose

7.3: O Divine One, who in the Great Auspicious Sacrifice (resembling a Yagna) of Shumbha and Nishumbha satisfied the thirst of Ghosts and Fiends (attending Lord Shiva), restoring cosmic balance through righteous battle.

7.4: O Divine Mother Victory, victory to You, O Slayer of Mahishasura , O Beautiful-haired One , O Daughter of the Mountain (Parvati) .


The Destruction of Dhumralochana (Inner Delusion)
  • Nijahunkriti (Sacred Roar) The power of inner awareness that instantly dissolves confusion and fear.
  • Dhumralochana The "smoky-eyed" demon represents ignorance, confusion, and a clouded vision of reality. The Mother does not need physical weapons to destroy this mental fog; a single stroke of divine sound or inner awareness (Humkara) completely shatters the deluding forces that obscure truth..
The Drying up of Raktabīja (Endless Desires)
  • Raktabīja Symbolism The endless multiplication of thoughts, desires, and anxieties when fed by attention.Raktabīja represents our desires, negative thoughts, and egoistic tendencies. Every time one desire is met or struck down, a drop falls and multiple new cravings sprout up in its place. The Divine Mother stops this exhausting, endless cycle by completely consuming and drying up the root source of desire within the seeker.
The War of Shumbha and Nishumbha (Cosmic Alignment)
  • Battlefield Drying The stopping of emotional reactivity that fuels repetitive mental suffering.Shumbha and Nishumbha represent the deep-seated dualities of "I-ness" (attachment to the individual self) and "My-ness" (attachment to possessions). By destroying them, the Mother feeds and satisfies the elemental, transformative forces of the cosmos, channeling primal energy back into a state of pure, righteous balance (Shiva-Shiva)
The Ultimate Salutation (The Auspicious Mother)
  • Core Lesson When awareness is steady, reactive thought-proliferation collapses on its own. Even amidst the most intense cosmic warfare, the Mother remains eternally beautiful, composed, and unshakeable. Her mountain lineage symbolizes her eternal, immovable, and foundational presence as the source of all existence.

✍️ रक्ष रक्ष जगन्माते सर्वशक्ते जय दुर्गे।🙏

This verse speaks directly to the experience of overthinking. Some thoughts don’t need to be solved—they need to be witnessed.

The more attention we give to repetitive fears or desires, the more they multiply, like Raktabīja's endless forms.

Durga’s roar represents a shift in awareness where you stop feeding mental noise with reaction.

What you resist in the mind multiplies. What you witness dissolves.

Silence is not emptiness—it is the moment when the mind stops reproducing its own disturbances.

Quoted below is the passage about the Lord Shiva (verbatim from https://www.artofliving.org/in-en/navratri/the-end-of-evil) and https://www.facebook.com/watch/?v=1226149297784868:

The Story: The brothers performed severe penance to become invincible. After conquering the heavens, they encountered Goddess Durga. Smitten by her beauty, they demanded she marry them. She declared she would only marry whoever could defeat her in battle, ultimately destroying both of them and their armies

In spiritual philosophy, their characters represent negative psychological states that destroy inner peace:

Shumbha: Symbolizes ego ("I and mine") and the inflated sense of self that blinds a person to humility.

Nishumbha: Symbolizes self-doubt and the continuous doubting of others, as well as the insatiable craving for validation.
