Verse 14: कमलदलामल


14. Ajna Chakra radiating calm, the sensory noise transforming into sacred lotuses

कमलदलामल कोमलकान्ति कलाकलितामल भाललते

सकलविलास कलानिलयक्रम केलिचलत्कल हंसकुले ।

अलिकुलसङ्कुल कुवलयमण्डल मौलिमिलद्बकुलालिकुले

जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥ १४ ॥

14.1: O Divine Mother, on Your elegant, creeper-like forehead shines a tender, soft radiance, which is spotless and pure like a flawless lotus petal , and beautifully adorned with the curved crescent moon.

14.2: O Divine Mother, You are the ultimate abode of all divine grace and creative arts, and Your graceful, rhythmic movements play out like the beautifully ordered steps of a flock of majestic royal swans!

14.3: O Divine Mother, Your hair is adorned with clusters of blue lotuses swarming with buzzing bumblebees, which elegantly intertwine upon the crown of Your head with fragrant Bakula flowers, blending their swarms together!

14.4: O Divine Mother Victory, victory to You, O Slayer of Mahishasura , O Beautiful-haired One , O Daughter of the Mountain (Parvati) .


Anamika vs. Mauna Scenario

This verse is highly tactical for Anamika's transformation while working right next to Mauna in the village mud kitchen.

Verse 14.1 When Anamika rests her mind on this line, she is focusing on the Forehead (Bhāla) and the Crescent Moon (Kalā), which in yoga represent the Ajna Chakra (the seat of pure, calm intellect and the Witness state).

  • The Application While busy chopping vegetables or managing the hearth, instead of letting her forehead furrow with tension or irritation at Mauna's presence, Anamika internalizes this verse.
  • The Shift She visualizes her own inner space as smooth, stainless, and radiating calm, cool light like a moonlit lotus petal. By focusing on the cool, artistic serenity of the Goddess's forehead, Anamika's own facial expression and mental space soften from reactive judgment into effortless grace.

Verse 14.2

  • The Royal Swan Metaphor (Kalahaṃsa) In Indian philosophy, the swan is the ultimate symbol of the Paramahamsa (the liberated soul). A swan glides through water without a single drop sticking to its feathers, and it possesses the mythical power to separate milk from water. By anchoring herself as the swan, Anamika effortlessly filters out Mauna's heavy energy, choosing to walk through the shared room light, fluid, and completely untouched by the local environment.
  • The Swarm of Reactive Thoughts (Ali-Kula) When Mauna causes friction (being indifferent or organizing things incorrectly), Anamika’s mind can instantly become a hyperactive hive. Dozens of toxic, angry mental thoughts ('Why does she always do this? She is ruining my day') start buzzing loudly around her head, just like a swarm of wild bees

Verse 14.3 In that traditional mud kitchen, this verse acts as a profound meditation tool for Anamika to manage her internal sensory overload and reactive mental noise while standing directly next to Mauna:

  • Transmuting the Buzzing into Sweetness Instead of fighting the mental noise with frustration, Anamika looks at this verse. She realizes that the bees on the Goddess’s crown (Mauli) are not a nuisance—they are drawing nectar from the sacred blue lotuses (Kuvalaya) and fragrant flowers (Bakula). She tells herself: 'Let these buzzing thoughts of irritation be drawn back to the center of my awareness to be purified into sweetness.'
  • The Secret of the Crown (Mauli) By anchoring her focus on the Goddess's crown (Mauli), Anamika conceptually pulls her energy up from her reactive lower center into the cool, silent space of higher consciousness. She observes the chaotic chatter of her mind without participating in it. The wild, defensive thoughts naturally lose their sting, settling down into a calm, sweet, aromatic silence, leaving her completely unbothered as she quietly tends the hearth.

✍️ रक्ष रक्ष जगन्माते सर्वशक्ते जय दुर्गे।🙏

Blueprint for Anamika's transformation:

1. Demons as Internal Projections: The 'demons' (Asuras) are not physical monsters. They are our own deep-seated Vasanas and Samskaras—specifically the machinery of the ego (Asmita), prejudice (Dvesha), and psychological conditioning. In the shared kitchen, Mauna isn't the demon; Anamika’s defensive internal reactions, her pride in being 'the perfect seeker,' and her rigid conditions on how a friend or family should behave are the real Asuras she must slay.

2. The Mirage of the Ego (Maya): Demons are like a Mirage. Shankara uses the exact same metaphor (Mriga-trishna) to describe Maya. A mirage looks incredibly solid and terrifying from a distance. If Anamika fights the mirage (arguing with Mauna, stewing in silent speeches), she is engaging in a futile effort because you cannot defeat a shadow by punching it. But on close inspection—through ruthless self-study (Svadhyaya)—she realizes the anger has no independent reality. It is just a temporary wave of conditioning passing through her awareness.

3. Turning Inward and Upward (Ajna Chakra - Line 14.1): This is why the verse opens by praising the Goddess’s forehead adorned with the moon (Bhāla-late... kalā-kalita). The forehead is the Ajna Chakra, the seat of the neutral Witness (Sakshi). By intentionally relaxing her forehead and turning her focus 'inward and upward,' Anamika shifts her energy out of the survival-driven lower chakras (fear, defense, irritation) and anchors herself in the cool, spacious, moonlit stillness of pure consciousness.

The Rhythmic Grace of the Swan (Line 14.2): Once her energy is at the Ajna center, her physical actions naturally reflect the Kalahaṃsa (royal swan). She no longer clashes with the environment; she glides through it, gracefully executing her chores while completely detached from Mauna's local frequency.

The Convergence of Grace and Perseverance: This final point is vital. To break a deeply ingrained habit loop (like a lifelong pattern of getting defensive when hurt), willpower alone is rarely enough. You need Perseverance (Abhyasa) to keep pulling the mind back to the Ajna center every single time it slips, combined with Grace (Anugraha)—the total surrender of the local ego to the supreme awareness of Durga.
