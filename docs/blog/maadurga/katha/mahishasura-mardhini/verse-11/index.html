Mahishasura Mardhini

ॐ

Verse 11: अयि सुमनःसुमनः


Maa Durga - cool, soothing, moon-like face and captivating, bee-like eyes

अयि सुमनःसुमनःसुमनःसुमनःसुमनोहरकान्तियुते

श्रितरजनिरजनिरजनिरजनिरजनिकरवक्त्रवृते ।

सुनयनविभ्रमरभ्रमरभ्रमरभ्रमरभ्रमराधिपते

जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥ ११॥

11.1: O Goddess whose beautiful mind is united with a charming and captivating appearance (radiance).

11.2: Whose beautiful face puts to shame the cool, soothing beauty of the moon (which shines at night).

11.3:Whose beautiful, moving eyes conquer the dark, buzzing beauty of swarms of bees .

11.4: Victory, victory to You, the Slayer of Mahishasura, the Beautiful One with lovely tresses, and the Daughter of the Mountain.


Divine Connections Between Durga and the Moon.

While the Moon itself is personified as the male deity Chandra (or Soma), its nurturing, cooling, and rhythmic energies are deeply tied to the Divine Mother.the Moon is considered a celestial manifestation of the supreme feminine energy (Shakti), of which Goddess Durga is a primary form.

  • The Left Eye of Durga: In scriptures like the Devi Mahatmyam, Goddess Durga is called Triyambake (the three-eyed goddess). Her right eye represents the Sun (action/fire), her central eye represents knowledge, and her left eye represents the Moon (desire, cooling grace, and intuition).
  • Maa Chandraghanta The third of Durga's Navadurga forms is explicitly named Chandraghanta. She wears a crescent moon shaped like a bell on her forehead, symbolizing how the Goddess governs mental peace, emotional clarity, and time.
  • Maa Mahagauri The eighth form of Durga governs the planet Moon in Vedic astrology. Her radiant, serene complexion is frequently compared to the glow of a full moon.

From a spiritual and devotional perspective (within certain Hindu traditions), the Moon is indeed often associated with calmness, cooling energy, and feminine divine qualities. Some people poetically connect it with goddesses like Durga or other forms of Shakti, and see moon-gazing as a meditative or devotional act.

Shukla Paksha (waxing moon): from New Moon (Amavasya) to Full Moon (Purnima): It symbolizes growth, clarity, and increasing divine feminine energy

Krishna Paksha is more introspective Symbolically linked with letting go, inward focus, and dissolution It is not considered “inauspicious,” but more inward and quiet in tone.
