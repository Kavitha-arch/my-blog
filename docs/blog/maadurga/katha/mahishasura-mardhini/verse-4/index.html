Mahishasura Mardhini

ॐ

Verse 4: अयि शतखण्ड


4. Maa Durga - Destroyer of Chanda(Ego-Driven Chase) and Munda(Reactive Withdrawal)

अयि शतखण्डविखण्डितरुण्डवितुण्डितशुण्डगजाधिपते ।

रिपुगजगण्डविदारणचण्डपराक्रमशुण्डमृगाधिपते ॥

निजभुजदण्डनिपातितखण्डविपातितमुण्डभटाधिपते ।

जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥

4.1: O Divine Mother who cuts the enemy into a 100 pieces , into tiny atoms ; demolishes the enemy to the depths of doom , and who destroys Shunda and his army of elephants .

4.2: O Divine Mother who smashes the temples of the enemy’s elephants , who scatters the army of demon Chanda ; who displays mighty valour against demon Sunda , and whose lion‑vehicle strikes Munda’s forehead and destroys his elephant army .

4.3: O Divine Mother who with Her own arms delivers punishment , who hurls endless divine weapons ; who wields a brilliant sword like lightning and destroys Chanda & Munda along with their generals .

4.4: O Divine Mother Victory, victory to You, Slayer of Mahishasura , O Beautifully‑haired One , O Daughter of the Mountain (Parvati) .


Kali fought and decapitated both the Daityas Chanda and Munda, the two generals of Shumbh and Nisumbh . Because she slew Chanda and Munda, the Goddess earned the name Chamunda.
  • Chanda (Fierceness/Passion) Symbolizes the flickering, unstable, and unpredictable nature of the mind. It represents the ego's outward, passionate pursuit of fleeting worldly desires.
  • Munda (Shaved Head/Senseless Withdrawal) Symbolizes a negative state of withdrawal or vairagya (dispassion). Unlike genuine spiritual detachment, Munda represents a reactive closing-off due to hurt, ignorance, or depression.
  • Chamunda (Transcendence) The Goddess Chamunda represents the supreme spiritual awareness required to defeat these inner tendencies. By invoking this energy within, practitioners transcend the traps of fickle desires and ignorant withdrawal, ultimately achieving spiritual liberation (Siddhatva)

Chanda Vritti (Ego-Driven Chase): Desires for material goods or relationships become destructive when they are fueled by the ego. Instead of you owning the desire, the desire begins to own you, forcing you into an endless, compulsive chase.

Munda Vritti (Ignorant/Reactive Withdrawal): When these ego-driven desires fail, a person might reactively withdraw from the world—such as becoming a withdrawn person simply to escape pain and avoid sadness

The Core Philosophical Lesson: True spiritual growth does not mean running away from life's challenges. Both happiness and sadness arising from the Chanda and Munda Vṛtti are temporary states of mind. They must be observed objectively with true detachment (Vairāgya), rather than avoided through reactive withdrawal.

Durga reveals that both desire and withdrawal — happiness and sadness alike — are fleeting states, to be transcended through true detachment (Vairāgya).
