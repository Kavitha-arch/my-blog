Verse 15: करमुरलीरव


15. Maa Durga: Voice(Flute) when Free of Vasanas - sweeter than cuckoo

करमुरलीरव वीजितकूजित लज्जितकोकिल मञ्जुमते

मिलितपुलिन्द मनोहरगुञ्जित रञ्जितशैल निकुञ्जगते ।

निजगणभूत महाशबरीगण सद्गुणसम्भृत केलितले

जय जय हे महिषासुरमर्दिनि रम्यकपर्दिनि शैलसुते ॥ १५ ॥

15.1: O Divine Mother, You are endowed with a beautifully sweet and charming mind; the melody from the flute in Your hands vibrates and flows out with such exquisite cooing notes that it puts even the sweetest nightingale to total shame!

15.2: O Divine Mother, You are gathered together and mingling with the simple forest tribes, and You gracefully move through the mountain groves which are deeply resonated with joy, echoing with their beautiful, heart-captivating humming and singing!

15.3: O Divine Mother, You awaken Your own innate spiritual virtues within the throngs of simple, raw tribal forest women, endowing them with excellent and noble traits as You participate in their vast expanse of playful, divine sports!

15.4: O Divine Mother Victory, victory to You, O Slayer of Mahishasura , O Beautiful-haired One , O Daughter of the Mountain (Parvati) .


Tool for Anamika as she transitions from the silent mindfulness of Verse 14 into the territory of Speech and Internal Dialogue in Verse 15:.
  • The Trap Anamika is immersed in her pooja. Nearby, Mauna chews meat noisily with her mouth open, creating a disturbance that Anamika finds deeply offensive.
  • The Transformation (Kara-muralī-rava) When Anamika focuses on this verse, she realizes that the Goddess’s mind (Mañju-mate) is a source of beautiful, harmonious music. The flute represents the unconditioned throat center (Vishuddha Chakra)—the seat of pure, melodious communication.
  • The Execution Anamika stops generating harsh internal mental retorts. Instead of allowing her inner voice to sound like a weapon, she softens it to sound like the sweet melody of a flute. Even if she has to speak a functional sentence to Mauna ('Could you pass the clay pot?'), her words flow out with a natural, gentle, and unshakeable sweetness (Vījita-kūjita) that leaves absolutely no room for egoic friction.
  • The Pure Acceptance of Raw Reality (Pulinda)The Pulindas are the mountain tribes—simple, unrefined, and raw forest dwellers. They don't know sophisticated royal etiquette. In this case study, Mauna's behavior represents this raw, unrefined human conditioning. When Mauna slams a pot or acts in a way Anamika considers 'incorrect,' Anamika's spiritualized ego usually recoils in judgment. This line teaches Anamika that the Goddess does not run away to a pristine palace; She gracefully mingles (Milita) right there with the raw elements of life.
  • Transmuting the Kitchen Friction into a Sacred Grove (Nikuñja)The mud kitchen, with all its heat, smoke, and interpersonal friction, is Anamika's Śaila-nikuñja (mountain grove). Instead of viewing the shared space as a prison or a hostile environment, Anamika internalizes the word रञ्जित (Rañjita)—meaning colored with joy. She stops demanding that Mauna become a perfect, refined seeker. She accepts the messy, raw human setting completely.
  • The Shift in Inner ResonanceBy dropping her high-minded intellectual judgment, Anamika stops generating cold energy. She allows her presence to become like the Manohara-guñjita (the captivating humming of the forest). She performs her chores in a state of absolute, unconditional inclusivity. Mauna's raw actions no longer clash with Anamika’s peace because Anamika has expanded her heart to include the raw, tribal, unpredictable nature of human conditioning within her own pure awareness.

Tool for Anamika to dissolve her spiritual narcissism and hidden judgment right beside the cooking hearth:

  • The Mirror of the Maha-Shabari (The Tribal Maiden)The Shabari represents the rawest, most unrefined, and chemically unconditioned human state. She has no sophisticated yogic training or scriptural education. In this model, Mauna’s 'incorrect' behavior represents this unpolished Shabari energy. Anamika's ego looks down on Mauna because Anamika reads scriptures and considers herself a 'pure seeker.'
  • The Illusion of Moral SuperiorityThis line shatters Anamika's spiritual pride. It declares that the Goddess doesn't manifest Her virtues (Nija-guṇa) only in quiet meditation halls. She infuses Her absolute highest virtues (Sad-guṇa-sambhṛta) directly into the unpolished, messy, wild Shabari women. Anamika realizes: 'If Durga can reside in the raw, unpredictable nature of the forest dwellers, who am I to look down on Mauna’s behavior as spiritually inferior?'
  • Entering the Divine Play (Keli-Tate)Anamika undergoes a deep internal shift. She stops treating the shared environment as a heavy cross to bear. She stops checking if Mauna is doing things 'correctly.' She views the whole kitchen dynamic as Keli-Tate—a vast, unpredictable playground of consciousness. She lets go of her rigid expectations, joins the physical flow of the kitchen, and allows her inner awareness to light up the space, seeing the divine presence operating through both herself and Mauna, completely unbothered by external flaws.

✍️ रक्ष रक्ष जगन्माते सर्वशक्ते जय दुर्गे।🙏

15.1 anchors her speech and inner mental dialogue to a soft, musical frequency, cutting off sharp internal retorts before they shape into harsh energy.

15.2 reminds her that consciousness doesn't hide in a pristine sanctuary; it gracefully walks right among the raw, unpolished, and messy elements of life.

15.3 shatters her spiritual pride, revealing that divine qualities manifest through the unrefined and unpredictable Shabari energy, prompting her to view the kitchen chores as an active playground of consciousness (Keli).

The Deep Psychological Mapping of Your Realization

The Dirty Spectacles (The Pivot of Inward Observation): You correctly point out that the moment we spot a flaw or a klesha in another person, it must act as an instant trigger to check our own lens. If Anamika is wearing spectacles covered in the dust of irritation or self-righteousness, everything Mauna does in that kitchen will look dirty and 'incorrect.' By pausing and cleaning her own lens through introspection (Svadhyaya), Anamika shifts from being a reactive judge to a pure observer.

The Anatomy of the Blocked Flute (The Trapped Vasanas): A flute cannot play music if it is clogged with mud. In this case study, the 'mud' clogging Anamika's inner flute is her storehouse of subconscious vasanas—her silent resentment, her rehearsal scripts of how to put Mauna in her place, and her tight, heavy defensive energy. If she tries to speak or act while clogged with this hidden negativity, the output will inevitably be a discordant, jarring note of ego friction.

The Empty Flute (Venu - Line 15.1): This is why the verse opens by praising the Goddess’s flute melody (Kara-muralī-rava). To become a 'perfect flute,' Anamika must completely hollow herself out. Through meditation and the grace of Maa Durga, she surrenders her local desire to be 'right' or morally superior to Mauna. She empties herself of all judgment. When she becomes completely hollow, her personal ego drops away, allowing the divine consciousness (Chiti) to flow through her effortlessly.

The Music that Shames the Cuckoo (Lajjita-Kokila): When the divine energy passes through an empty, unblocked instrument, the resulting expression is automatically flawless, gentle, and infinitely beautiful. Anamika no longer has to force herself to 'act nice' or put on a fake smile for Mauna. Because her internal space is completely clean and hollowed out, the positive energy radiating from her words, her micro-movements, and her silent presence is so sweet and pure that it naturally puts all environmental friction to shame, transforming the heavy kitchen atmosphere into a sacred, harmonious grove.
